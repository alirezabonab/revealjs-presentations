Notes · 45 s

One idea. You reach for max when you cannot describe the path, only the goal, and you are handing the approach over rather than the task. The flaky test the room saw a moment ago is exactly that: no reproduction, no obvious place to start.

Then the cost of that freedom, in the vendor's own words. Given room to explore, it will sometimes explore past the point of usefulness. On the other five tasks the room saw, the path was already clear, so there was nothing to hand over and nothing to gain.

Keep these in your mouth, not on the slide: 15% of coding attempts in one measured set ran into the output cap and none were solved, so a long run fails late rather than gently. And what actually protects you is something that can check the answer, not a higher setting.

when it pays

Use it when the work is too big for one context, breaks into independent pieces, and each piece can be checked on its own. That ran 55% cheaper than the big model working alone.●

when it loses

It loses when the steps depend on each other and the whole job fits in one context. You pay for a plan, a handoff and a merge that one model does for free, and the single model won every time it was measured.●

when it is wasted

It is wasted on one file, on a prompt you have not thought through, and on ordinary daily coding. If one engineer could finish it in an afternoon, one model can too.
